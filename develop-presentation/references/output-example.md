# Content output example

An example `Content` section holding an outline. Adapt the headings and leave out what does not apply.

```markdown
## Content

### Outline
1. Establish the task: use a familiar drafting workflow to identify what
   the tool changes and what remains a human responsibility.
2. Compare stage and total time: show why a large drafting reduction can
   produce a smaller overall reduction.
3. Apply the distinction: ask participants to choose a drafting task and
   name the checks needed before using its output.
4. Close: identify what to measure in a real trial before generalising.

### Evidence and qualifications
Source: invented teaching figures, not an observed trial. Drafting falls
from 30 to 12 minutes; review and approval stay at 60. With the stages in
sequence, totals are 90 and 72 minutes: (30−12)/30 = 60% less drafting
time; (90−72)/90 = 20% less total time. Quality is not measured.

### Continuation
Real performance claims need measured evidence. This request ends with
the content plan; slide production is a possible next task.
```

For a storyboard, expand a scene only as far as the request needs.

```markdown
### Scene: compare the workflow
Audience task: tell the drafting reduction from the total reduction.
Takeaway: “Total time falls from 90 to 72 minutes.”
Evidence: invented teaching figures; label them “illustrative figures”
beside the comparison.
Visible: both workflows, stage values and totals together in a small table.
Spoken: explain the unchanged review stage and the limits of the illustration.
Reference: the values, calculations and assumptions, for readers.
Transition: “What would we need to measure in our own workflow?”
Check: ask which reduction applies to drafting and which to the total.
```
