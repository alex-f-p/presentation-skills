# Storyboard and channels

## Sequence

Order the content by the audience’s questions and what they must know first. Open with why it matters and enough context to begin; add a biography or agenda only if the audience needs it. Each transition says why the next question follows. The close delivers the outcome in the brief.

## Scenes

Plan scenes, not finished paragraphs. Keep objects together when the audience must compare or combine them. Note visual needs that affect the argument, such as a whole-system view beside a detail. Leave positions and typography to design.

## Channels

Decide what each channel carries:

| Channel | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| Live slides | Evidence to inspect, relationships, exact wording, labels and orientation. |
| Speech and notes | Reasoning, interpretation, guided inspection, transitions and description of visuals. |
| Reference material | Full procedures, methods, definitions and sources. |
| Reading version | The rationale and navigation the absent speaker would have supplied. |

- Text on a live slide earns its place as a label, recurring term, recap or caption. Move extended text that repeats the speech into notes or reference material.
- Notes serve readers only if recipients can open them. A live deck plus a usable reference is enough; a second deck is not required.
- Test reading material with no narration.
- Keep qualifications that change a claim’s meaning in every version the audience sees.

## Time

Budget time for reading, comparing, activities, questions, demos and recovering from problems. Give the audience pauses to absorb an idea and a stable frame of reference to return to; adding slides or reveal states does not fix pacing.

When content is revealed in steps, keep or restore what the audience needs for comparison, including in static exports. Treat timings as estimates until a run-through is observed.
