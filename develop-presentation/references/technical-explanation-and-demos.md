# Technical explanation and demos

## Explanation

Judge subject knowledge, graphical literacy and language separately. Introduce an unfamiliar term, axis, mark or notation before asking the audience to draw a conclusion from it. Give specialists a brief orientation and newcomers a schematic or worked example; include a tutorial only when the audience needs one.

- **Mechanisms:** Explain each component and what each connection means: flow, control, dependency or time. Show how a detail maps to its place in the whole system.
- **Code and equations:** Keep the definitions, assumptions and dependencies the inference needs. Supply full runnable code or derivations separately when the audience will need them.

Test the result: can a newcomer trace the process, and can a specialist verify it?

## Demos

1. Plan the input, the action, the result and the conclusion the audience should draw.
2. Explain decisions; do not tour features.
3. Check dependencies: access, data, state and network.
4. For anything that can fail live, prepare a captured result or other fallback, and say plainly when the audience is seeing prepared material.
5. Keep recovery cues in presenter material.
6. Allow time for loading, inspection, questions and recovery. Cut scope if it does not fit.

Plan captions and descriptions of essential visuals. Stable examples and labels help people who look away to rejoin.
