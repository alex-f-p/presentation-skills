# Composition and reveals

## Titles

Title each slide by what it holds:

| Slide holds | Title |
| --- | --- |
| A finding | The takeaway, stated as far as the evidence supports it. |
| An open question | The question. |
| An artefact, such as a screenshot or diagram | A precise label. |

A claim in a title needs the same evidence, scope and uncertainty as a claim anywhere else.

## Layout

- Give the audience one place to start and a path to follow.
- Size emphasis by importance.
- Let the layout follow the information: aligned columns for comparison, a path for sequence.
- Align items that are compared, and put labels next to what they label.
- Use whitespace to separate groups, never at the cost of shrinking essential content.
- Keep terms, units, colour meanings, type roles and notation consistent across the deck.
- Where colour carries meaning, add a second cue such as a label, shape or position.
- Check the smallest labels and strokes the audience needs in the rendered slide. A font size or word count is a starting point; only the render shows whether it is readable.

## Reveals

Reveal content in steps that match the explanation. Leave time to inspect each step, and keep a stable frame of reference between steps. When the audience must compare or combine, keep the whole visible or bring it back.

A plain cut is enough for most steps. Use motion only to show trajectory, timing, change or how something operates, at a pace the audience can follow.

In exports, check that each page shows a complete view or a meaningful step, with no half-built slides or overlapping transition states.

## Furniture and player

Check masters, footers, handouts and player controls against the editorial rules and the brief’s numbering and navigation requirements. If the player adds controls that conflict, find a presentation mode or export that does not, and report any limit that remains.
