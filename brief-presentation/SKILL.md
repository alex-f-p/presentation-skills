# Brief a presentation

Produce a concise brief: what should this audience understand, decide or do, and under what conditions?

## Read first

- [Audience and use](references/audience-and-use.md) for mixed audiences, access needs, recordings or more than one delivery mode.
- [Editorial rules](references/editorial-rules.md) if the brief sets presentation style or includes wording the audience will see.

## Steps

1. Start from what is supplied and keep choices already made. Ask only about gaps that change the outcome, audience priority, evidence, format or feasibility, and keep working on whatever does not depend on the answer. For smaller gaps, state an assumption.
2. Cover what applies:
   - Audience: prior knowledge, concerns and likely misunderstandings.
   - Outcome, and an observable sign that it was achieved.
   - Use: live, discussion, remote, recorded or reading; time and interaction.
   - Constraints: viewing conditions, language, access, voice, branding and format.
   - Evidence available, scope, exclusions and open questions.
3. Turn topics into outcomes. “Introduce the architecture” might mean the audience can trace a request, or can approve a change.
4. Fit the outcome to the time. Allow for explanation, reading, discussion and recovering from problems. If it does not fit, narrow the outcome; never plan to talk faster.
5. Check that another reader could say what success is and what is out of scope.
6. If the audience, outcome or use has changed, revise the parts of the brief that depend on it and name the later work affected.

## Rules

- Promise only what the evidence can support.
- Keep facts, assumptions and preferences apart.
- Stop at the brief unless the request includes more.

## Output

Return the brief in the form requested; a paragraph can be enough. Chat-only requests stay in chat, and a small advice request needs no file.

For a saved project, write to its working document, or to `presentation.md` beside the presentation files. Read the existing sections first, update `Brief` and keep settled decisions. See the [output example](references/output-example.md) for structure. End with a continuation note: what remains within the request, and what changed that affects later work.
