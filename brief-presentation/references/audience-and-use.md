# Audience and use

## Audience

Judge subject knowledge, graphical literacy and language fluency separately. Infer needs from the invitation, earlier discussion and supplied context, never from job titles or nationality. State what you assumed.

| Situation | Planning consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Decision-maker and advisers | Identify who decides and what the others must contribute. |
| Mixed expertise | Establish shared prerequisites and optional depth; keep the detail needed to verify claims. |
| Familiar subject, unfamiliar evidence | Explain the measure or convention; skip the general background. |
| Critique requested | Include assumptions, methods, alternatives and open questions. |
| Conflicting audience needs | Name the priority, or raise the unresolved choice. |

## Use

| Use | Plan for |
| --- | --- |
| Live | Time to read, compare and ask questions. |
| Discussion meeting | Returning to evidence out of order. |
| Remote | A small window, and caption overlays that cover part of the slide. |
| Recorded video | Accurate, synchronised captions for meaningful audio, and descriptions of essential visuals the audio does not convey. A transcript is a reading alternative; it does not replace captions. |
| Recorded audio | A transcript. |
| Independent reading | The rationale and navigation the speaker would have supplied: an annotated deck, notes or a separate reference. One of these is enough. Confirm recipients can open the notes. |

## Access

Plan for access needs even when none are reported: readable detail, defined terms, visual descriptions and time to process. Check scripts and reading direction for the audience’s languages. Stable examples and persistent labels help people who look away to rejoin.

## Evidence

Check what the evidence can support before setting the outcome. Keep observed results, proposals and invented examples apart. Understanding in the room, later retention and adoption at work each need their own evidence.

Record only the conditions that change the presentation.
