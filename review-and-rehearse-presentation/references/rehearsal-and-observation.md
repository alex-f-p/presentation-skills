# Rehearsal and observation

## What each input supports

Conclude only what the input can show.

| Input | You can |
| --- | --- |
| Slides or outline | Identify audience workload and likely pacing risks; estimate a run-through. |
| Script or notes | Check planned speech, transitions and pauses. A timed read-through counts only if someone performed it. |
| Transcript | Check what was said. It shows nothing about voice, visual timing or how the room understood. |
| Audio | Observe spoken explanations, pauses and duration. Judging coordination with slides needs synchronised slide evidence. |
| Video or synchronised recording | Inspect visible states and delivery at timestamps. |
| Audience or peer response | Report what those people understood or misunderstood on that task. |

Never invent body-language observations, successful reruns or audience responses.

## Planning a run-through

Give time to every part: opening, explanations, pauses for reading, reveal states, demos, activities, transitions and questions. For a demo, include setup, recovery and a fallback, with cues in presenter material. Have the speaker describe essential visuals in their own words, not read the slide aloud.

## During and after delivery

Record elapsed time at each section boundary. Expect unevenness: one comparison can need minutes while several simple reveal states pass quickly.

| Observation | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| The speaker has to improvise an explanation | Revise the prerequisites or the representation. |
| Alternatives disappear before the audience has compared them | Restore them. |
| The talk overruns | Cut or restructure lower-priority content. If that is not enough, revisit the outcome. |

## Reporting

Record the revision, time allowed, observed duration, conditions, timestamped findings, changes and recheck results. Name the venue and access conditions nobody tested.

Keep observed and estimated apart. “The recording lasts 18:20 against 15 minutes” is observed; “this cut may save two minutes” is an estimate until another run.

Fit performance advice to this speaker and what you observed; do not apply general body-language conventions.
